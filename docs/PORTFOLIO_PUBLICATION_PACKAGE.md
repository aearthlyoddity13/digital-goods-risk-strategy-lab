## 1. Publication decision

Publish P1 as **one flagship work page**. Do not split short drama, AI services, the engine and the interactive lab into separate portfolio projects. They are mutually exclusive layers of one system:

1. **General framework** — the payment-platform decision problem.
2. **Category applications** — short-drama coins and AI subscriptions/credits.
3. **Decision evidence** — synthetic scenario results and policy comparisons.
4. **Product proof** — the interactive strategy lab and API.
5. **Governance** — methodology, limitations and production requirements.

This structure demonstrates breadth without presenting four partially overlapping projects.

### 2.3 Executive summary — the 60-second layer

**Section label:** `01 · DECISION PROBLEM`  
**Heading:** `Digital-goods risk is interaction-driven.`

**Copy:**

`Small tickets do not necessarily mean small platform exposure. A merchant can settle funds while customers still hold unused coins or credits; value can be consumed before a dispute arrives; and content, account-security or service-continuity failures can interrupt fulfillment without first appearing in payment-loss rates.`

`The strategy therefore evaluates what can go wrong, what protection already exists and which control keeps residual exposure within appetite at the lowest sustainable cost to the merchant and platform.`

**Decision principle callout:**  
`Use the least-restrictive control that keeps residual risk within appetite.`

### 2.4 Decision architecture — general framework

**Section label:** `02 · DECISION ARCHITECTURE`  
**Heading:** `From merchant evidence to an explainable action.`

Present five numbered stages:

1. **Establish the boundary**  
   `Check legitimacy, sanctions and prohibited-activity constraints before commercial optimization.`
2. **Diagnose risk mechanisms**  
   `Separate payment conduct, fraud, contingent exposure, content/dependency, cross-border and maturity risk.`
3. **Measure protection independently**  
   `Evaluate tenure, service reliability, support, liquidity and evidence quality without allowing strength to erase risk.`
4. **Compare control economics**  
   `Estimate residual exposure, approval opportunity, operating cost and merchant liquidity burden across three postures.`
5. **Return an accountable decision**  
   `Provide controls, reserve treatment, reasons, release conditions and escalation triggers.`

**Supporting sentence:**  
`The system recommends balanced growth when it is within risk appetite and economically near-equivalent to a more restrictive eligible posture.`

### 2.5 Category deep dives — independent applications

**Section label:** `03 · CATEGORY APPLICATIONS`  
**Heading:** `One framework, two distinct exposure structures.`

#### Short-drama coin systems

**Summary:**  
`Short drama compresses acquisition, coin purchase, episode unlock, consumption and dispute into a short interval. The key platform question is whether unused purchased value, post-consumption disputes and viral cross-border growth jointly create losses the merchant cannot absorb after settlement.`

**Mechanisms to show:**

- purchased-coin obligation and ledger reconciliation;
- post-consumption dispute evidence;
- monetization and renewal clarity;
- content-rights and continuity interruption;
- viral growth before cohort quality is observable.

**Policy conclusion:**  
`Track purchased and promotional coins separately, retain episode-level fulfillment evidence and link reserves to the uncovered obligation gap—not to category labels alone.`

**Expandable link:** `Read the short-drama research note`

#### AI subscriptions and credits

**Summary:**  
`AI services combine subscription conduct, prepaid credits, usage metering, account or API-key compromise, service reliability and variable compute exposure. The correct response depends on whether risk is customer-wide, account-specific or linked to an outstanding credit obligation.`

**Mechanisms to show:**

- trial, renewal and cancellation conduct;
- purchased, consumed, refunded and unused credits;
- usage-meter reconciliation and bill shock;
- account and API-key compromise;
- provider dependency, reliability and postpaid exposure.

**Policy conclusion:**  
`Contain localized key or account abuse before restricting the entire merchant, and connect reserve or processing controls to measurable release conditions.`

**Expandable link:** `Read the AI-services research note`

### 2.6 Scenario evidence — what changed and why

**Section label:** `04 · SCENARIO EVIDENCE`  
**Heading:** `Stress the mechanism, not the category label.`

Lead with three selected scenarios; do not publish the full 32-row catalog on the main reading path.

| Scenario | Synthetic change | Decision result | Strategic lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viral cross-border short drama | Volume, cross-border share and outstanding obligations rise faster than evidence | Controls → manual review; 5% → 10% reserve | Growth increases uncertainty; combined exposure requires temporary protection. |
| Post-consumption dispute deterioration | Complaints, disputes, integrity concerns and unused value worsen together | Controls → manual review; 5% → 16% reserve | Interaction effects can matter before a severe aggregate score is reached. |
| AI account/API-key abuse | Fraud, anomalous usage and disputes concentrate during an abuse event | Approve → manual review; 0% → 10% reserve | Target the abuse mechanism, then relax controls after verified remediation. |

**Disclosure:**  
`Dollar results use a configurable synthetic merchant with $1 million in monthly attempted payment volume. They are illustrative, not benchmarks.`

**Expandable content:** “View all scenarios” may reveal the remaining outcomes in an accordion or linked appendix.

### 2.8 Findings — executive synthesis

**Section label:** `06 · FINDINGS`  
**Heading:** `Five conclusions from the system.`

1. `Interaction effects can be more decision-relevant than isolated thresholds.`
2. `Unused customer-funded value is contingent exposure, not automatically an expected loss.`
3. `Merchant strength should reduce uncertainty without erasing observed risk.`
4. `Restrictive controls should be mechanism-specific, temporary and tied to release conditions.`
5. `The strongest control is not automatically the best strategy once approval opportunity and merchant liquidity are considered.`

## 4. Experience and visual specification

### Layout

- Use a 12-column desktop grid with a maximum content width of `1440px` and fluid gutters.
- Keep long-form copy at `60–72ch`; allow evidence tables and the interactive lab to span wider.
- Use a sticky **right-side** section navigator on long screens. It appears after the hero enters the scroll history and never overlaps content.
- Navigator labels: `Problem`, `Architecture`, `Applications`, `Evidence`, `Lab`, `Findings`, `Build`, `Method`.
- On narrower screens, collapse it into an unobtrusive progress indicator and section menu.

### Visual language

- Preserve the portfolio’s Obsidian Chrome system: onyx base, cool blue-slate elevation, alabaster primary text and restrained warm-metal accent.
- Use serif display type for the thesis and findings; use a precise sans for interface copy and a monospaced face for metadata and model labels.
- Avoid generic card grids. Use ruled editorial sections, asymmetrical column spans and one dominant analytical surface—the lab.
- Use color semantically: blue slate for information, muted green for within-appetite, amber for watch/review and restrained red only for hard boundary or severe stress.

### Motion

- Use motion to explain sequence and state, not to decorate.
- Reveal the five decision stages in order as they enter view; `400–600ms`, staggered by `60–90ms`.
- Animate scenario deltas and economic bars only on first entry or user-triggered state change.
- Let the right-side navigator track reading progress with a smooth active-state transition.
- Use subtle mask or clip reveals for section headings; no scroll-jacking, perpetual parallax or delayed access to content.
- Button and control feedback: `150ms`; accordions/tabs: `250ms`; contextual panels: `400ms`.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; all content must remain available with animation disabled.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

- A recruiter can state the problem, method and principal finding after a 60-second scan.
- Every public number is labeled synthetic or illustrative at its first meaningful appearance.
- The page contains no draft labels, internal notes, incomplete placeholders or unsupported predictive claims.
- Short drama and AI services read as independent applications of one general framework.
- The interactive lab loads, handles API failure and remains usable by keyboard.
- The section navigator displays complete labels, sits on the right and does not cover content.
- Motion is visible but never blocks reading; reduced-motion behavior is verified.
- Desktop QA covers Chrome, Edge and Safari; responsive QA covers at least `1440`, `1280`, `1024`, `768` and `390px` widths.
- All public links, canonical metadata, OG content and schema markup use the production domain.
