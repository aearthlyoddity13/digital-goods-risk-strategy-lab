# Research question tree

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision-maker:** Employer-neutral digital payment platform  
**Geography:** United States centered, with cross-border considerations

## Primary question

How should a digital payment platform onboard, monitor and control emerging digital-goods merchants while balancing fraud and contingent exposure against sustainable merchant growth and payment volume?

## Scope

Comparative coverage: short drama, web fiction, games and AI services.  
Deep dives: short-drama coin systems and AI subscriptions/credits.  
Payment channel: direct web checkout only; app-store billing is outside the modeled payment flow.

## MECE question structure

### Q1. Is the merchant and business model sufficiently legitimate and understandable?

- Can ownership, operating entities and regions be verified?
- What exactly is sold: access, subscription, coin, credit, virtual item or usage?
- When is fulfillment complete?
- Which customer, creator or supplier obligations remain after payment?
- How dependent is the merchant on one app store, social platform, content supplier or AI-model provider?

**Primary decision stage:** onboarding and periodic review.

### Q2. Does the product and customer journey create avoidable conduct risk?

- Are price, renewal, cancellation, refund and credit terms clear?
- Can a customer distinguish one-time, recurring and auto-recharge payments?
- Does advertising match delivered access or performance?
- Are support and refund channels functional?
- Are virtual goods transferable, expiring, refundable or cash-like?

**Primary decision stage:** onboarding, product review and remediation.

### Q3. What payment and fraud mechanisms can create loss?

- Stolen credentials and card testing
- Account or API-key takeover
- Device, account and payment-instrument reuse
- Promotion and multi-account abuse
- High-velocity or automated purchasing
- Friendly fraud after consumption
- Refund and subscription abuse
- Transaction laundering or undisclosed business activity

**Primary decision stage:** authorization strategy, monitoring and escalation.

### Q4. What contingent exposure remains after settlement?

- Unresolved dispute and refund exposure
- Unused coins, credits and subscription value
- Creator or supplier obligations where relevant
- Merchant negative-balance capacity
- Growth and volume volatility
- Settlement timing versus loss emergence
- Interruption risk from content, app-store or model-provider events

**Primary decision stage:** reserve, settlement delay and processing limits.

### Q5. What content, IP and cross-border constraints could interrupt the merchant?

- Content rights and repeat infringement indicators
- Restricted or harmful content
- AI digital-replica, privacy and copyright concerns
- App-store or distribution-policy violations
- Sanctions and restricted-jurisdiction exposure
- Mismatch among merchant, customer, content and payment geographies

**Primary decision stage:** enhanced review, geographic controls and offboarding.

### Q6. Which control package produces the best risk-adjusted outcome?

- Expected loss prevented
- Uncovered contingent exposure
- Approved and successfully fulfilled payment volume
- Platform contribution after losses and operating cost
- Merchant liquidity burden
- False-positive and review friction
- Conditions for reducing or escalating controls

**Primary decision stage:** portfolio optimization and control review.

## Required outputs

Every synthetic assessment must return:

1. Decision posture.
2. Risk-family assessment rather than one unexplained total.
3. Merchant-strength assessment.
4. Commercial-value assessment.
5. Primary drivers and protective factors.
6. Recommended control package.
7. Illustrative reserve or settlement action when relevant.
8. Conditions to reduce controls.
9. Escalation triggers.
10. Data availability and confidence.
11. Methodology version and synthetic-data disclosure.

## Originality and evidence boundary

- External sources support factual claims and documented mechanisms.
- The question tree, taxonomy, indicator prioritization, control logic and synthetic scenarios are original project work.
- Internal payment-platform signals are represented only as synthetic variables.
- No threshold is described as optimal or production-calibrated.
